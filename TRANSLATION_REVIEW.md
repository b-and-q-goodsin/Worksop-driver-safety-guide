# Translation review brief

## For the reviewer
You are checking a safety notice for lorry drivers arriving at a UK distribution centre. The English is the management-approved text, so meaning must match it exactly. Open your language's sheet from the `translation-review` folder in Excel. Compare each draft column with the English master, fill in your name and language, mark Y or N, and write the corrected wording where needed.

Check in particular that:
- every instruction says what the English says, including small words: rule 2 says vehicles "should" take extra care (advice), rules 3 and 4 say "always", and rule 9 says the yellow fixed chocks are driven over in suitable vehicles
- rule 17 says site staff place the chocks and the driver checks they are removed
- a driver cannot read any rule as the opposite of what is meant
- words are what a professional driver in that language would use

To test on a phone, scan the reviewer QR card, or open the live address with `?review=1` on the end. Tap your language, read each page, then tap Read this page to listen.

If the text changes after sign-off, any recorded audio for that language must be recorded again.

## Languages in this round
French, Romanian, Polish, Russian, Bulgarian, Ukrainian, Lithuanian, Latvian, Portuguese (European), Spanish, German and Turkish. All are drafts. Lithuanian, Latvian, Bulgarian and Turkish need an especially careful reviewer, and every reviewer should be a fluent speaker who knows how lorry drivers talk.

## Differences from the gatehouse sheets (owner to be aware)
1. Language choice is the first screen, so a driver does not read English first.
2. The welcome note, the three acknowledgement boxes and the thank-you line are page wording, not from the sheets. They are needed for the page to work.
3. Goods-In item 1 says "while your vehicle is being tipped". The sheet reads "while your being tipped", which looks like a typing slip.
4. Goods-In item 3 reads "your hi-vis gives 360-degree visibility" for "your hi vis is 360 visibility".
5. Rule 12 names the subject (passengers, pets or children) in the sentence, because the sheet gives it only in the title.
6. Headings say "Goods-In" with a hyphen.
